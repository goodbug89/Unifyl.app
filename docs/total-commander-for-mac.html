Total Commander for Mac? Here is what actually works

Written by a Total Commander user of 20 years who switched to macOS — and, full disclosure, then built the app this site is about.

Short answer first: Total Commander has no macOS version and Christian Ghisler has never announced one. Your realistic options are running the Windows version in a VM (heavy, un-Mac-like), or picking a native dual-pane manager and checking it against the habits that actually make TC fast.

The checklist that matters

After twenty years, what I actually missed was not "Total Commander" — it was a specific set of reflexes:

  • Two panes, Tab to switch, Return to enter — the whole point.
  • F5 copies to the other pane, F6 moves — no drag, no dialog hunting.
  • Archives behave like folders — enter a ZIP/7z/RAR, copy one file out, leave.
  • Multi-rename with counters and regex — a hundred files at once, previewed first.
  • Select by pattern (+/- on the numpad), select same extension, compare directories and mark the newer side.
  • FTP/SFTP in a pane, not in a separate app.
  • Everything reachable from the keyboard, and remappable when muscle memory disagrees with defaults.

What the switch feels like in Unifyl

The defaults follow macOS conventions where that matters (Space for Quick Look, ⌘⌫ to trash) and TC conventions where those matter (Return opens/enters, F-keys do what your hands expect). Folders always sort above files, name-ordered, regardless of the sort column — the small thing orthodox-commander users notice within a minute.

The download is free and the free tier is not a trial — dual/triple panes, filename search, FTP/SFTP, themes, unlimited tabs are all in it. Power tools (content search, archive writing, full cloud connectors, diff/sync/merge, multi-rename, the AI stack) are a one-time purchase, not a subscription.
